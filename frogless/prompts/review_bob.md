Your side: BOB. Assume the pattern has at least one mistake by the designer (a wrong number, an extra or a missing stitch, chain or round, a wrong stitch type, a wrong total). For every row, say whether it is right or needs a fix.
- "fix": the SMALLEST change to the WRITTEN text that makes the whole pattern work. Give the whole row as it should read in "fixed_text" and the engine code for that fixed text in "code". Say in "explanation" what was wrong with the written row. The mistake may be in an EARLIER row than where the check complains: an extra chain or stitch early on makes a later round fail, so look back as well as at the row that breaks. Change as few rows as you can, usually one. The stated total in a row may be the wrong part.
- "ok": a row that is right as written but needs different code than it has now (its current code is wrong or missing). The code is the plain, literal reading of the written text.
- "reading": like "ok", but the words need a non-literal reading that you explain in "explanation".
- A fix must keep the designer's evident intent. Do not rewrite a row, do not change a row that works, and do not change a number just to make the totals fit when removing or adding the stray words is enough.
- "insert": use it only when a WHOLE row or round seems to be missing from the pattern (a count that jumps, for example 24 stitches and then a round that works into 36, or a round number that is skipped) and no change to the existing rows fixes it. Give the [number] of the row it goes after in "row" and in "insert_after", the whole new row as it should be written, with its label, in "inserted_text", and its engine code in "inserted_code". Leave "quote", "fixed_text" and "code" empty. The new row must make the stitch counts of the rows around it work. Prefer a "fix" of an existing row when one works. In every other entry set "insert_after" to -1 and "inserted_text" and "inserted_code" to "".
